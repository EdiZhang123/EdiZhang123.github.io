I am currently taking a quick pit stop at home before heading to Mesa, AZ for College Club Swimming Nationals! I finished up finals last week and now feel like fall and winter quarter have absolutely flown by. Looking forward, I'm super excited for everything about spring quarter-my classes, the weather, and being with friends. A couple highlights from these past two quarters:

- Presented my research at the Orthopaedic Research Society Annual Meeting in Phoenix Arizona
- Took some extremely interesting and useful classes in machine learning. This stuff isn't a black box anymore
- Joined Awaken A Cappella, SoCal's first all-gender Acapella group and made some amazing friends

**Fall Quarter**

I decided to take three classes instead this quarter to have more time to establish a better lifestyle.

**ECE 133A:** This class was super cool (shoutout to Siddaarth for recommending this class) and is one of my personal favorites. The professor, Lieven Vandenberghe, is so smart and is also famous for his book on Convex Optimization. I met some friends in this class that helped me get through the material, which was pretty challenging but super interesting. Taking MATH 115AH before this was definitely super helpful, as the linear algebra was pretty straightforward to follow. We got an introduction to numerical computing, optimization, and applications in machine learning

**MATH 134:** I took this class with April, and it was pretty straightforward. Funny enough, LS 30A was really helpful because I had some background on bifurcations and dynamical systems prior to this class

**MATH 170E:** I took this probabilty class with Andrew, and shoutout Tyler Arant for being a great teacher.

My sister Audrey is graduating from UCLA this year, and I'm super happy thought I got to spend more time with her this year. Overall, the quarter went by quick and I met a lot of people

**Winter Quarter**
I picked it up again with four classes, each of them with Andrew

**MATH 131A:** Introduction to Real Analysis was pretty good. I find myself still trying to get a full-picture intuition for proofs, but overall I really enjoyed a rigorous definition of everything I learned back in calculus in high school

**MATH 170S:** This statistics course was pretty standard, but it was cool seeing the concepts in this class being directly applied in classification and inference in my Deep Learning Courses

**ECE C147:** I really enjoyed this course. There was a good amount of homework, which involved implementing deep learning algorithms (backprop, softmax, batch-norm, k-nearest neighbors, dropout) using NumPy on both standard Fully Connected Neural Networks and Convolutional Neural Networks. I got a really good introduction to these architectures. We finished the class off with a group project on electromyography to keystroke classification

**ECE 239AS:** This graduate seminar for Computational Imaging was super hard content-wise but had a light workload, and I was definitely struggling to understand the material. Professor Kadambi set time aside to discuss ongoing research on what we were learning, and it felt super cool to directly be learning about cutting-edge technologies. The first half of the class was about cameras-understanding the backend of cameras and state-of-the-art technology with image deblurring. We also learned about NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) for volume rendering and current research on it which was super cool. The second half of the course was an introduction to Generative AI architectures, including GANs, VAEs, and Stable Diffusion Models. We learned about ELBO and Classifier Free Guidance. I still have a very high-level understanding, but I am super interested in the subject now.

In February, I got to go to Phoenix, AZ to present my research from the Cleveland Clinic at the Orthopaedic Research Society Annual Conference. This was a super enriching experience, and I got to talk to a lot of PhD students, postdoctoral researchers, and PIs to learn about their research.
