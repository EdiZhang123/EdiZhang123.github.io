Sitting at the end of winter quarter of my junior year, it suddenly dawned on me how little time I have left at UCLA. If I had to drop out of college now or graduate this year, would I feel fulfilled in my college experience? I've been really starting to think about what my takeaways from UCLA are beyond my degree, because did I experience everything to the fullest? And is the pursuit of fulfilling all of my idealistic experiences too quixotic? On one hand I feel like I cracked the code for maximizing my time college. On the other hand I feel like I could have done so much more.

For a period of time I lamented on how there were months that I wasted on things that didn't materialize into some tangible benefit to my life, whether it was living each day without intention, drifting from my hobbies, or investing in relationships/friendships that ultimately didn't really bring the best out of me. I've come to realize that in this era of my life, many experiences actually carry the same weight such that I ultimately discover something new about myself, how I interact with others, and what I enjoy/care about.

I've started to reshape my perception of what contributes to my social currency. From virtually the start of my education, the notion of my career and my grades being the single defining factor of my success pushed me towards the traditional path of chasing prestige, never feeling satisfied, and believing all my worth came from my achievements. I know this is the cliché eye-opening life crisis that every Asian kid goes through eventually, but I also think I'm only partially there, as I still feel like I'm not fully living my life for myself, but for the image that people have of me. For much of my life I've always followed and trusted what those close to me believed to be right for me, and that has led to me very good place, but it has also left me questioning what my purpose is to myself. As close as I am to my parents, my sister, and my friends, nobody truly knows me in every aspect, and because of that I must dictate what I do in my life. It's easy for me to say this, but I find myself deferring to other people to help me make decisions for myself, which I am trying to work away from. I wish for the dynamic to be seeking advice and consultation rather than counting votes for what others think I should do and deciding off of an election where I don't even put my own ballot in.

I think about everything I've been able to experience in college. The main highlight has to be joining many organizations with dramatically different people. I made friends with future consultants, engineers, bankers, musicians, teachers, actors, doctors, entrepreneurs, and so much more, and I've learned so much from interacting with vastly different people. I've also developed my skills in making new connections and talking to new people, which I found to be so rewarding and eye-opening. There are a couple of things still on my college bucket list: the first is studying abroad and traveling around the world, and the second is playing in a musical. The most enriching experiences I've had in my life all came from a spontaneous willingness to try something I previously saw as trivial or a waste of time.

That being said, I think the answer to my first question is no, but it will always be no-and I say no with not an ounce of regret. I believe if I were to say yes then I wouldn't have set my bar high enough, but nevertheless I take each of my wins with pride and gratitude.
