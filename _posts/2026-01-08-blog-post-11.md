I just finished up my winter break Asia Trip, which was 23 days total. I visited nine cities in four countries, had delicious food/drinks from about 70 different places from nice dinners to street food, and went to many historic sites and beautiful parks. I was able to get the cost of my entire trip at almost exactly $4000, which included all flights, food, transportation, shopping, random fees, etc (every penny that came out of my bank account). It was quite exhausting but a genuinely amazing experience. This was probably the most logistically complicated trip I've ever planned (including transfers, I had 7 flights!).

In the past year, I've traveled extensively, and I feel like these experiences helped me realize a few things about both myself and the purpose of seeing the world. Here are the places I went to (some of these are copied from summer recap blog):

  I came here in February for the Orthopaedic Research Society Annual Conference and in April for Club Swim Nationals

  Only stayed here for one day since I had a 21-hour layover, and it was absolutely freezing. The street food looked really tasty, but unfortunately I had no cash on me.

**What Travel means to me:**

I identify as a traveler and not really a vacationer. I found my travel experiences to be truly enriching in the different experiences I've been able to have-whether that be places I hiked, food I ate, or conversations I had with strangers. But I think something less cliche that I've started to see about traveling (especially solo traveling) is that not every moment is meant to be super fun, but rather a unique challenge to navigate unfamiliar environments and make real-time decisions. Being a college student with a budget, my strong desire to see as much as possible really forced me to get really efficient with my spending while maximizing my experiences.

- **Problem Solving:**

  I ran into so many situations where I was lost and confused trying to understand how different things worked in different places. For example, this included Japan's insanely convoluted public transportation system, figuring out how to enjoy my one day in Seoul without withdrawing any cash, communicating with people through a language barrier in a non-touristy place, and many more. Figuring out a foreign country's transit system with little English help was so incredibly difficult, but after talking to random people that were so kind to help me out, I ultimately made it work. These kinds of experiences give me confidence in my ability to solve a problem despite how uncomfortable I am.

- **How can you make the most out of your time?**

  Traveling at 20 is a once-in-a-lifetime experience. I have the freedom of being a college student, energy of a teenager, and probably the highest tolerance for modest living and transportation conditions I'll ever have in my life. Because of this, I have an itch to try as many things as possible in the time I have. I feel like these experiences have made me less picky and more open-minded.

- **Seeing how other people live**

  I get this feeling most when I go to Asia since my family is from there, and in an alternate life where my parents didn't immigrate to America I would have grown up in a small city in China and spent my entire childhood preparing for the Gaokao. The difference in freedom that I grew up with compared to what my cousins grew up with was so clear to me. I'm using freedom broadly too-freedom to play a sport in high school, freedom to pursue extracurricular activities I enjoy, freedom to travel to so many places visa-free. It makes me increasingly grateful each day for the opportunities I've been blessed with, such as U.S. citizenship, American schools, and attending UCLA.
